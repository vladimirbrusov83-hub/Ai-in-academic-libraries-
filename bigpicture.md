# Big-picture take

The **idea** is genuinely strong and the **structure** is the most polished part. The **topic coverage** is mostly right but has two real gaps. Detail below.

## The idea

**Strong.** You're filling a real, documented hole:

- Pulse of the Library 2024 says 32% of librarians have no AI training — that's your TAM, and you cite it on the homepage.
- ACRL only released the AI Competencies framework in October 2025, so anything mapped to it sub-competency-level is genuinely first-wave.
- "Practitioner voice from a working community college librarian" is the right wedge. Every other resource in this space is either vendor marketing (Clarivate, EBSCO, Ex Libris webinars), academic R1-flavored, or an ALA whitepaper. A reference desk voice from a teaching-focused institution is underrepresented and matches the Charleston-audience honesty principle: real numbers, honest failure, peer-to-peer tone.
- The Level 3 bet (vibe coding, agentic AI, workflow automation for librarians) is the actual moat. Levels 1–2 will get commoditized within 12 months as every library school and vendor ships a "ChatGPT for librarians" intro. Level 3 is what no one else is shipping.

**Risk to the idea:** the half-life on AI content is brutal. A module written today about "what AI does well" is partially wrong in 9 months. You need a visible "last reviewed" date per module and a process for refreshing — otherwise the ACRL-aligned credibility erodes fast.

## The structure

**Three levels × 16 modules, mapped to ACRL sub-competencies** is the right backbone. What works:

- Foundations → Applied → Advanced is the same arc every successful tech curriculum uses (think Carpentries, freeCodeCamp).
- Audience paths (practicing vs. digital librarian) acknowledge that a metadata librarian and a reference librarian have different jobs. Most library AI content pretends one curriculum fits everyone.
- 16 modules is the right ceiling — enough to feel comprehensive, not so many it looks like an unfinished textbook.
- Sub-competency-level ACRL mapping is your credibility anchor with directors and tenure committees. Keep this visible.

**Structural gaps:**

1. **No assessments or "show you can do it" component.** Charleston-style honesty notwithstanding, librarians taking this for CE credit or to justify professional development funds need *something* to point at. Even a self-check quiz per module or a capstone deliverable (e.g., "draft your library's AI use policy") would make this defensible as PD.
2. **No time-to-completion estimate at the curriculum level.** Each module has `estimatedMinutes` — sum it and put it on the curriculum page. "16 modules · ~6 hours total" is what a director needs to approve staff time.
3. **No clear path between audiences.** A practicing librarian who wants to grow into systems work has no signposted route. The two paths currently feel parallel, not connected.
4. **"Coming soon" for half of Level 3 is risky.** Charleston audiences are skeptical of unfinished promises. Either ship Level 3 module 12 (vibe coding) as a working draft, or reframe Level 3 as a roadmap rather than locked-behind-email.

## Topics covered

**Well-covered:**
- Mental models for LLMs (what it is, hallucinations, probabilistic output) — module 01 is genuinely good
- Prompting, tool selection, critical evaluation
- ACRL ethics competency
- Reference, instruction, research support use cases
- Metadata/cataloging angle for digital librarians
- The Level 3 advanced bets (vibe coding, agents, automation)

**Gaps I'd add or surface more aggressively:**

1. **AI and collection development / acquisitions.** This is the Charleston Conference's #1 topic and you have zero modules on it. AI for license review, vendor negotiation, OA agreement tracking, weeding decisions, demand-driven acquisition. If you want this curriculum picked up by Charleston attendees, this is the missing module.
2. **Copyright, licensing, and AI training data.** You touch this inside module 01 but it deserves a standalone module. This is the single most-asked question from faculty right now: "Can I use ChatGPT with my unpublished manuscript?" Librarians need a defensible answer.
3. **Patron-facing AI policy and reference desk scripts.** What do you say when a patron asks "is ChatGPT okay for my paper?" A module of *scripts and decision trees* would be the most-shared thing on the site.
4. **AI literacy instruction for students.** Distinct from "AI for librarians" — this is "how to teach an AI literacy one-shot." Instruction librarians live and die by this. Probably belongs in Level 2.
5. **Privacy, FERPA, institutional data.** What happens when a librarian pastes a student's research question into ChatGPT? This deserves more than a paragraph inside the ethics module.
6. **Vendor AI features (Primo Research Assistant, EBSCO AI Insights, Scopus AI, JSTOR's tool, Clarivate's Research Assistant).** Librarians are being sold these *right now*. A "how to evaluate vendor AI claims" module would be wildly useful and uniquely aligned to the Charleston publisher-library audience.
7. **Cost, budget, and procurement.** Every Charleston talk eventually circles back to money. What does ChatGPT Team cost per librarian? Is it worth it? Who pays — the library or IT? This is missing entirely.

**Topics I'd consider trimming or merging:**
- The split between "AI types: generative, predictive, agentic" in module 01 and a separate agentic AI module in Level 3 has overlap. Make sure they're complementary, not redundant.
- "Picking the right tool" can date very fast — frame it as a decision framework, not a tool comparison, or it'll need updates every quarter.

## One-paragraph summary

The idea is right, the structural skeleton is right, and the practitioner voice is the asset. The two things that would make this 2× stronger are: **add the acquisitions/collections/vendor-AI dimension** (it's where the money and the Charleston-style audience actually live), and **ship Level 3 even in rough form** because that's the only part no one else has. Everything else — assessments, time estimates, refresh dates — is polish on a solid foundation.
