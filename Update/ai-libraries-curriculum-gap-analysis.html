AI in Academic Libraries — Internal Planning Document

Curriculum Gap Analysis & Revision Plan

Based on research brief: The State of AI in Academic Libraries, 2025–2026 · Current curriculum: ai-in-academic-libraries.vercel.app · June 2026
1 — Gap analysis by level
Level 1: Foundations — Modules 01–05
01
What AI actually is
Add RAG and agentic AI concepts — ACRL (Oct 2025) explicitly requires these in the Knowledge & Understanding competency category. Current framing predates the standard.
update
02
Talking to AI effectively
Core skill gap confirmed by research: 66.5% of librarians use AI but most are solo ChatGPT users with no structured approach. Module concept is sound; add a prompt-library on-ramp pointer to M10.
ok
03
Picking the right tool
Tool landscape has shifted materially. Ex Libris Primo Research Assistant (GA to all Primo customers), OCLC Dec 2025 cataloging AI, and ProQuest Research Assistant are all now in production and not yet reflected in the module.
update
04
Ethics, copyright & policy
Three major new anchors: ALA draft guidance (Apr 2026) on vendor transparency and patron privacy; Library Copyright Alliance Oct 2025 statement asserting existing copyright law is adequate; ARL/CNI Mar 2026 futures report. All post-date current content.
update
05
Critical evaluation of AI output
Add the ACRL "right to refuse" framing — AI literacy should not imply an obligation to adopt. Add RACBAC (Relevance, Accuracy, Coverage, Bias, Authority, Currency) as a concrete evaluation framework, published in the International Journal of Librarianship 2025.
update
Level 2: Applied — Modules 06–11
06
AI for research support
Add vendor discovery tools in production: Primo RA, Summon RA, JSTOR AI, EBSCO AI Insights, Ebook Central, Scopus. Note AI detection unreliability per ACRL K&U competency. Elsevier's Scopus-grounded agentic Deep Research is now a relevant demo case.
update
07
Reference & instruction
Add ChatGPT-essay critical evaluation exercise (widely cited library instruction case study). Community college and first-generation learner framing is entirely absent — significant gap given the site's stated CC librarian voice.
update
08
Metadata & cataloging
OCLC Dec 8 2025 announcement — AI cataloging in WorldShare Record Manager and Connexion suggesting DDC/LCC/LCSH — and BFAS §3.5 update for recording AI-generated metadata provenance are directly on-topic and not yet reflected.
update
09
Digital collections & discovery
Specto for digital collections, Alma AI Metadata Assistant, and the Ex Libris Academic AI platform are now GA. Module concept is solid otherwise; add a section on AI de-duplication (OCLC WorldCat test, Feb 2025).
update
10
Prompt library for library work
Research confirms this as the most-requested, most-underserved skill area among practicing librarians. Module is well-positioned. Add role-specific prompt sets for CC librarians as a differentiated deliverable.
ok
11
Making the case to administration
Budget has overtaken skills as the #1 adoption barrier (62% overall, 66% for academic libraries, Clarivate 2025). Reframe from skills-advocacy to budget justification and ROI argument. Add vendor-AI evaluation checklist as a learner deliverable — aligns directly to ALA draft guidance language.
update
Level 3: Advanced — Modules 12–16
12
Automating repetitive tasks
Solid concept; only fully published Level 3 module. Research confirms automation of metadata, drafting, and discovery summarization is the top implementation target across libraries. No content gaps — ships as-is.
ok
13
Agentic AI: what it means
ACRL Oct 2025 formally defines agentic AI in K&U. ARL/CNI Mar 2026 adds a governance layer: authorization, auditability, containment, quality control. Write governance in from day one — not as a patch. Module ships June 15.
update
14
Vibe coding for librarians
Research confirms this is a clear gap across all practitioner-facing library training. UW iSchool now teaches low-/no-code AI tools in its MSIM AI specialization. Custom GPTs and LibGuide chatbots cited as natural practitioner entry points. Position and framing are correct.
ok
15
AI & library systems integration
Sound module concept. Needs a vendor-AI due diligence section: data privacy, black-box risks, environmental cost, contract and licensing questions — per ALA draft guidance language on vendor transparency.
update
16
Your AI strategy & next steps
Research documents that librarians are systematically under-represented in institutional AI policy-writing. Add policy co-authorship as an explicit learning outcome alongside the existing professional identity framing.
update

4 — Priority order for implementation
Timing flag: ALA draft guidance goes to Council at the June 2026 ALA Annual — likely this month. If adopted, M04 needs a same-week content update to reflect the adopted version, not the draft. Monitor ala.org before finalizing M04.
1
M04 — Ethics, copyright & policy
ALA draft guidance closes at the June 2026 ALA Annual. If adopted, this becomes the second mandatory anchor alongside ACRL. High credibility risk if content is visibly pre-2026 when learners check references.
medium lift
2
M03 — Picking the right tool
Ex Libris Primo RA, OCLC Dec 2025 cataloging AI, and ProQuest RA are all GA. Any learner cross-checking the tool list finds it stale immediately. Highest near-term discoverability risk.
low lift
3
M08 — Metadata & cataloging
OCLC Dec 8 2025 announcement and BFAS §3.5 provenance update are the biggest cataloging AI development of the year. Digital librarian audience will notice absence. Content is well-defined and additive.
low lift
4
★ New: Vendor AI due diligence (M11a)
Largest unmet need in the field per research. No existing practitioner curriculum covers vendor evaluation, patron privacy, environmental cost, and contract questions at this depth. ALA draft guidance provides a ready-made teaching framework. High differentiation value.
high lift
5
M13 — Agentic AI + governance
Ships June 15. ACRL names agentic AI explicitly. ARL/CNI Mar 2026 adds the governance framework (authorization, auditability, containment, quality control) that makes the module actionable rather than just definitional. Build it in from the first draft.
medium lift
6
M01 — What AI actually is (+RAG / agentic)
ACRL requires RAG and agentic AI in K&U. Foundational module sets learner expectations for everything downstream. ~200-word additive section; low effort for full ACRL alignment.
low lift
7
M07 — Reference & instruction (+CC lens)
The site's author is a CC librarian. The CC / equity framing is entirely absent from M07 and is the most differentiated angle available to the curriculum. Adds both credibility and audience specificity simultaneously.
medium lift
8
★ New: CC / equity track (M11b)
Research identifies CC and instruction-focused librarians as the most underserved population. No existing practitioner curriculum has this track. Long-term: consider a CC learning path toggle on the curriculum homepage.
high lift
9
M11 — Making the case (+budget ROI reframe)
Budget overtook skills as the #1 barrier in 2025 (Clarivate). Current framing is skills-advocacy. Reframing around ROI and budget justification is a single revision pass and directly addresses what administrators are hearing.
low lift
10
M05, M06, M09, M15, M16 — remaining updates
All are additive content updates, not structural rewrites. Batch after priority items 1–9 are shipped. Within this group, M16 (policy leadership reframe) has the highest standalone value.
low lift
