## TASK 2: ARL Guiding Principles — Verified

**Title:** Research Libraries Guiding Principles for Artificial Intelligence
**Published:** April 2024
**Source:** https://www.arl.org/resources/research-libraries-guiding-principles-for-artificial-intelligence/

Seven principles confirmed via secondary sources (PDF is canonical):

1. Foster Digital Literacy
2. Understand and Raise Awareness of AI Bias
3. Advocate for Openness and Transparency
4. **No Human, No AI** — consequential decisions must not be delegated entirely to AI
5. Prioritize Security and Privacy
6. Copyright Flexibility (libraries have a stake in preserving rights for nonprofit research)
7. *(Seventh principle in PDF — text not recovered from secondary sources)*

**Curriculum impact:** "No Human, No AI" (Principle 4) is a strong framing device and was added explicitly to Module 04 content.

## TASK 3: Competitive Landscape

### LibTech Insights / Clarivate Micro-Course

**Exists. Well-documented. Direct competitor for Levels 1–2 content.**

- Full name: "Generative AI Literacy Essentials for Academic Librarians"
- Partners: Choice (ACRL) + Clarivate / LibTech Insights
- Launch: Mid-July 2025
- Format: Free, 8-module, newsletter-based, self-paced
- Certificate on completion
- Explicitly follows ACRL AI literacy framework

**Differentiation strategy:** Their course is newsletter-format, conceptual, and institutionally toned. No hands-on tool building, no workflow automation for library staff, no vibe coding. Our differentiator is: structured path + practitioner voice + the only portal that teaches librarians to build their own tools.

### OCUL "AI Tools for Academic Libraries" Series

**Exists. Bimonthly blog posts on Choice360, not a course.**

Published installments cover: AI Research Assistants, Speech-to-Text Models, Programming and Coding Tools, Task Management, Video Creation. Notably one post covers "AI Programming and Coding Tools" — the only library-sector content adjacent to vibe coding — but it is a single article evaluating tools, not a curriculum for building with them.

| FIU | Confirmed — most comprehensive | Workflow integration page, ACRL framework tab, widely adapted by other institutions |

## TASK 4: Market Statistics — Updated

### Pulse of the Library 2024 (Clarivate, September 2024)
- **69%** of academic libraries evaluating, planning, or implementing AI (the "60%+" framing in the brief is accurate)
- **32%** of librarians report no training available at their institution
- **43%** of U.S. respondents report no training available (32% is the global figure)
- 47% cite tight budgets as greatest challenge
- Survey period: April–June 2024

### Pulse of the Library 2025 (Clarivate, October 2025) — Most Current
- **67%** of libraries exploring or implementing AI (up from 63% overall in 2024)
- **U.S. optimism about AI is strikingly low: 7%** vs. 27–31% in Asia and rest of world
- Regional divergence: Asia and Europe at 37–40% in initial implementation or beyond; U.S. at 14–16%
- 28% have formal training/onboarding programs (updated framing of training gap)

**How to use these stats in the portal:**
- "69% of academic libraries" is more precise than "60%+" — use the precise figure, cite "Pulse of the Library 2024 (Clarivate)"
- The 32% no-training stat is from 2024 and remains the most specific figure for that gap — attribute specifically
- The U.S. confidence gap (7% optimism) is powerful new data for Module 11 (Making the case to administration)

## Curriculum Changes

### Change 1: Sub-competency codes added to all modules

The original curriculum used broad category labels (Ethics, Knowledge, Application, Analysis). The actual ACRL framework has 20 specific sub-competencies. Each module now maps to specific sub-competency codes.

**Why:** Specific sub-competency alignment is what gives the portal credibility with ACRL-aligned institutions. The Clarivate micro-course uses broad labels; we use precise codes. This is a concrete differentiator.

### Change 2: Market statistics updated

- "60%+" updated to "69% of academic libraries" (2024 figure, more precise)
- Added 2025 Pulse figure: 67% exploring/implementing (most current)
- 32% no-training figure retained and attributed to 2024 survey specifically
- Added U.S. vs. global optimism gap (7% vs. 27–31%) for use in Module 11

### Change 3: Module 04 — ARL "No Human, No AI" principle added

The ARL's fourth guiding principle states that consequential decisions must not be delegated entirely to AI. This is added explicitly to Module 04 content as a core framing concept.

**Why:** This principle is both authoritative (ARL, 2024) and practitioner-relevant. It's a memorable phrase that gives librarians a policy anchor for discussions with administrators.

### Change 4: Module 12 — Ex Libris LibOW acknowledged

Ex Libris Library Open Workflow (LibOW) is a no-code drag-and-drop automation platform for Alma/library systems. It connects to OpenAI, Microsoft 365, and GitHub. It is a commercial product (not a course) that targets non-programmer library staff.

This is referenced in Module 12 as existing context: librarians at Alma-using institutions may already have access to this. Module 12 teaches the underlying concept so learners can evaluate any such tool critically.

### Change 5: Module 14 differentiation sharpened

HKU's vibe coding LibGuide is acknowledged in the module as the only prior library-sector content — and the distinction is made explicit: HKU's guide explains the concept for students; this module teaches the skill for library practitioners building library tools.

**Why:** Honest acknowledgment of adjacent content strengthens credibility. The distinction (student-facing conceptual overview vs. practitioner-facing applied curriculum) is genuine and significant.

### Change 6: Module 11 — U.S. confidence gap data added

The 2025 Pulse data showing U.S. librarians at 7% optimism vs. 27–31% globally is powerful context for making the case to skeptical U.S. administration. This data point reframes the conversation: U.S. institutions are behind their global peers, not just behind a trend.

**Why:** Module 11's audience is librarians advocating to skeptical leadership. This data makes the case without editorializing.

### No modules added or removed

The 16-module structure is appropriate. No gaps warrant new modules; the Level 3 modules cover the identified differentiated territory. No modules overlap significantly enough to merge.
