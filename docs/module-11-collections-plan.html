Implementation Plan

Module 11 — AI for Collections & Vendor Evaluation

A new Level 2 (Applied) module, inserted in reading order. Curriculum grows 16 → 17 modules.

Level 2 · Applied Audience: All Librarians Inserted as #11

Context

The curriculum has zero coverage of collection development / acquisitions and of the patron-facing vendor AI products librarians are being sold (Primo Research Assistant, Scopus AI, EBSCO AI, JSTOR, Clarivate). Per bigpicture.md, this is "where the money and the Charleston audience actually live."

Module 15 covers vendor contract due-diligence from the systems angle (and even references a never-created vendor-ai-due-diligence slug); Module 09 is digitization/discovery, not collection building. So the gap is real.

Decision: ONE combined module, tagged "both" (All Librarians), placed in Level 2 in proper reading order — inserted as id 11, before "Making the case to administration." This renumbers later modules and shifts several id-keyed maps. Total: 16 → 17.

Proposed: slug ai-for-collections-vendor-evaluation, title "AI for collections & vendor evaluation", level: "applied", audience: "both", acrlCompetencies: ["analysis","application","ethics"].

Part A — Write the module content (the main effort)

Follow the CLAUDE.md writing voice and the 6-section prose shape of modules 09–11 (~3,500–4,500 words, ~20 min). Research first for current accuracy on vendor AI products, pricing, and collection-dev use cases — AI content dates fast. First-person practitioner voice throughout; each section ends on where human judgment stays irreplaceable.

Suggested sections

  1. Where AI fits in collection development — selection, vendor title-list review against policy, weeding/deselection signals, demand-driven acquisition.
  2. AI for acquisitions & licensing ops — OA agreement tracking, license/contract summarization.
  3. Evaluating vendor AI products (Primo Research Assistant, Scopus AI, EBSCO AI, JSTOR, Clarivate) — what they actually do, where they fail, how to test claims with your own queries.
  4. Cost, budget & value — per-seat costs, who pays (library vs. IT), is it worth it. The gap Modules 11 & 16 only gesture at.
  5. Patron-facing & privacy considerations specific to vendor AI search tools.
  6. A decision framework: adopt / pilot / decline a vendor AI feature.

Plus intro, a specific practitionerNote, 5–6 summary bullets, objectives, topics. Add a moduleReferences[11] entry (APA 7th, real sources).
