AI Fluency for Library Instructors

A bonus module integrating Anthropic's AI Fluency for Educators framework into the library curriculum — covering the 4D Framework, instruction session design with AI, and academic integrity modeling.

Context

Anthropic's "AI Fluency for Educators" playlist (4 videos) introduces the 4D Framework — Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence — and educator-specific AI strategies directly applicable to librarians who design and teach instruction sessions. The course already cites Anthropic's AI Fluency course but doesn't use the 4D Framework by name or incorporate educator-specific techniques. This content works best as a focused standalone bonus module rather than being distributed across existing published modules.

Source Material (4 Transcripts)

1

Introduction to AI Fluency for Educators

Overview of the course, why AI fluency matters in teaching, the role of educators in shaping responsible AI engagement. Key framing: "How do we bring AI into our teaching in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical, and safe?"

2

Summary of the AI Fluency Framework

Full breakdown of the 4D Framework: Delegation (problem awareness, platform awareness, task delegation), Description (product/process/performance types), Discernment (three evaluation levels; recognizing genuine insights, not just errors), Diligence (creation, transparency, deployment).

3

Applying AI Fluency to Course Design

Building rich shared context as the foundation of AI partnership. Three fundamental design tasks: identifying content, mapping the learning journey, articulating objectives. The "AI as student focus group" technique. Journalism/statistics case study.

4

Applying AI Fluency to Learning Materials

Creating worksheets, assessments, handouts using accumulated context. Verification checklist. Diligence statements. The academic integrity challenge — honest framing. Transparency diligence as a teaching opportunity.

Module Content Outline (6 Sections)

1

The 4D Framework for library instruction work

Name and define Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence with library-specific examples for each. Frame this as the mental model underlying effective AI collaboration in teaching contexts.

Source: Video 2
2

Building shared context with AI as your instruction partner

How to brief AI on your student population, session constraints, course context, and institutional environment. Moving from generic assistant to contextualized colleague. Example: one-shot session for first-year comp students vs. research methods seminar for grad students.

Source: Video 3
3

Three core instruction design tasks AI can support

Identifying content and concepts for a session. Mapping the learning journey (sequence, pacing, scaffolding). Articulating learning objectives tied to student motivations. All three illustrated with library instruction examples.

Source: Video 3
4

AI as student focus group

Ask AI to role-play as one of your students at different points in a planned session. Use this to surface confusion, test whether instructions are clear, identify where examples land poorly. Practical prompt structure for library instruction contexts.

Source: Video 3
5

Designing learning materials with accumulated context

Creating handouts, worksheets, and activities that build on accumulated session context. Product/process/performance description taxonomy applied to material creation. Verification checklist: accuracy, bias, learning barriers, scaffolding coherence. Diligence statements — brief disclosures about AI use in materials shared with students or faculty.

Source: Video 4
6

Academic integrity, AI, and what librarians can model

Honest framing: AI can complete many assignments at passing level — this is the landscape students and faculty navigate. Librarians' unique position: not to police, but to model transparent, responsible AI collaboration. Showing your own AI use process as an information literacy teaching moment. ACRL Framework connection: Authority is Constructed, Scholarship as Conversation — human expertise remains the authoritative layer.

Source: Videos 1 & 4
